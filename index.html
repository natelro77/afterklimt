Memory Fragments

Fragment 001

2023 — 02:14 AM

The laugh came first, before anything else. Before the name, before the voice, before the whole sentence that made the laugh necessary. It stayed longer than it should have.

She laughed like she already knew the punchline.
Fragment 002

2023 — undated

There is a particular kind of attention she gives — not performative, not searching for an exit. Just present. The kind that makes you want to be worth it.

I wrote three drafts of the message before sending nothing.

Some doors open before you realize you were reaching.
Fragment 003

2024 — early spring

The way she moved through a room. Not making an entrance. Just — arriving.

I noticed immediately and said nothing for twenty minutes.

Both things can be true.

Some observations require no elaboration.
Fragment 004

2024 — 11:58 PM

She reads things slowly. Which means she actually reads them. That is rarer than it should be.

She would understand this, actually.
Fragment 005

2024 — no timestamp

There were small things she did that she probably didn't know she was doing.

The tilted head. The specific silence before she said something important. The way she held her own opinion quietly, then stated it once clearly and let it stand.

I have thought about this more times than I am comfortable admitting.

The smallest gestures have the longest half-lives.
Fragment 006

2024 — 03:41 AM

I kept finding gold long after she left a room. That isn't a metaphor. It is also a metaphor.

The trace of someone who was genuinely there.
Fragment 007

2024 — summer, Tuesday

This is not a love letter. This is a record.
A careful, very slightly obsessive record.

Of someone who deserved to be noticed, and was. Even if she never heard about it.

Especially because she never heard about it.

Room IV  —  The Observatory

2:43 AM

A room for thoughts that are only possible at this hour.
The kind that arrive quietly and refuse to leave.

Some people change the temperature of a room by entering it. Not loudly. Just — the air adjusts.
There is a specific loneliness in knowing someone well enough to miss the version of them you never fully got to see.
I thought about saying it at least four times. Each time, the moment passed before I could decide whether it was the right one.

I think that was cowardice. I am filing it here as “restraint.”
Distance is strange. It doesn’t always grow with miles. Sometimes the person is right there and the distance is still very large and you are still very aware of it.
